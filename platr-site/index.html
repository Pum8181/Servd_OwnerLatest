QR Ordering Built in Burnaby, BC

Your kitchen never sees an order until a person checks it first.

Most QR ordering tools send an order straight to the kitchen the second a guest taps submit, typos and all. Servd adds one extra step: every order sits in a confirmation queue first, so a real person can catch the double tap, the wrong spice level, or the order that was meant for table 6 and landed on table 9. Guests ordering from the same table get tracked individually, and splitting the bill afterward takes one tap instead of a server working it out by hand.

How A Table Becomes A Paid Check

Four steps. No app. No POS overhaul.

01

Guest scans and orders

They scan the table's QR, see your live menu, pick spice levels, and tag their name, all from their own phone, no app to install.

02

It pauses for you

The order lands in your "Needs Confirmation" queue, kept clearly separate from anything already headed to the kitchen.

03

One tap, it's confirmed

Review it, fix anything wrong, tap Confirm. It's timestamped, and now it's genuinely in your kitchen's queue.

04

Bill however they want

Together, split evenly, or split per person, chosen in one tap, and displayed clearly enough to read aloud at the table.

Why I Built This

I got tired of watching small restaurants get treated like an afterthought.

I'm Pankaj Singh, a software developer and designer based in Burnaby, BC. I started building Servd after watching a few independent restaurants near me get boxed into ordering platforms that were clearly designed for chains: steep per-order fees, generic branding, and support that meant filing a ticket and waiting.

None of that made sense for a twelve-table restaurant run by one family wearing five different jobs on a Friday night. So I built something smaller on purpose. No commission on your sales, no call center, and no bloated feature list you'll never open. Just the two things that actually matter at a busy table: someone checking the order before it hits the kitchen, and a bill that splits the way your guests actually want it to.

I design it, build it, host it, and support it myself. If something breaks at 7 PM on a Friday, you're not sitting in a ticket queue. You're texting me.
